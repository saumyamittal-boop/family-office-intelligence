# Financial-engine methodology, assumptions and limits

> Illustrative simulation, not investment advice. Results depend on the data, assumptions, and methodology used. Historical outcomes do not
> guarantee future performance.

This page explains what the engine in `fo/engine.py` does, in the order it does it, and what it cannot tell you. The **assumption values** are
listed once, in [protected-assumptions.md](protected-assumptions.md) (frozen by a golden-master test; changing one needs the project owner's
approval). Decisions behind the method: ADRs [0004](adr/0004-constrained-mean-variance.md), [0005](adr/0005-drawdown-by-historical-replay.md),
[0006](adr/0006-rule-based-liquidity-tiers.md), [0007](adr/0007-risk-metrics-and-scenarios.md), [0011](adr/0011-data-sources-and-handling.md) and
[0013](adr/0013-illiquid-fixed-and-modelling-conventions.md). **Every number is a draft placeholder, not an externally validated estimate.**

## 1. What is modelled

A family has a **corpus** (in rupees crore) split across five market asset classes (large-cap equity, mid-cap equity, debt, gold, cash and
liquid) plus an **illiquid** bucket (property, business, private holdings). It has annual spending, dated one-off outflows, a **drawdown limit** (the
worst fall it can tolerate), a **real-return target**, a **planning horizon**, a **safety-net window** in months, and minimum and maximum
limits per class. The engine proposes three mixes (Growth, Balanced, Defensive), measures them, and stress-tests them.

## 2. Data

By default the engine uses **synthetic daily returns** generated by `fo/data.py::make_sample_returns` (random seed 42, 2,609 business days from
2016 to 2025, Student-t noise with fixed correlations, plus a stylised 2020-style crash and rebound and a 2022 rate shock, re-centred so each
class's average annual return equals a set target). They resemble nothing in particular and are **not** NSE, AMFI or RBI data. Their averages
are a property of how they were generated, not evidence about any market.

If a file `data/snapshot/daily_returns.csv` exists (a date column plus one column per class), the engine reads it instead and the API reports the
source as `snapshot`. **That path is a loader only**: no snapshot is shipped, no data feed exists, and nothing checks a snapshot's quality,
licence or fitness (ADR-0011 lists what a real snapshot would need first). The API's `/api/meta` shows which source is in use.

## 3. Estimation

1. Daily returns are compounded to **monthly** returns.
2. The **covariance** is estimated from the monthly returns, with the **correlations shrunk towards zero** by the Schaefer-Strimmer intensity (a
   data-driven weight between 0 and 1) while each class's own variance is kept; it is annualised by 12.
3. **Expected return** per class is a 50/50 blend of the sample's average (daily mean x 252) and a fixed long-run **prior** per class. The priors
   are judgement placeholders (docs/personas-and-assumptions.md).
4. The **illiquid bucket** takes its return, volatility and correlation with equities from the profile (0 correlation with the other classes) and
   is added to the covariance as a sixth asset. It is **held fixed**: only the five market classes are optimised ("the liquid sleeve").

## 4. Allocation

For a risk-aversion value lambda the engine maximises `expected return - lambda/2 x variance` over the liquid sleeve's weights, subject to:

- weights add up to 100% of the sleeve;
- each class stays between its minimum and maximum (shares of the sleeve; the reported weights are shares of the whole corpus);
- the **liquidity cover** is at least the amount the family needs (section 6), when that is achievable.

It is solved with SciPy's SLSQP. If the family's limits make the required cover impossible, the best achievable cover is used and the result is
flagged (`liquidity_infeasible`). Whether the solver reported success is carried to the output as `optimiser_converged`; in all four demo families
every solve converges.

## 5. Risk levels and the drawdown limit

- **Three levels.** Instead of fixed lambda values (which can all land on the same corner), each level is defined by *where its volatility sits*
  between the lowest-risk mix and the highest-return mix the limits allow: **Growth 75%, Balanced 50%, Defensive 25%** of the way. The lambda
  for each is found by bisection.
- **Drawdown.** The chosen mix is replayed through the daily history at constant weights (daily rebalancing, no costs, illiquid bucket accruing
  smoothly) and its worst peak-to-trough fall is measured. If that exceeds the family's limit, lambda is multiplied by 1.35 and the mix is
  re-solved, up to 30 times. If no step fits, the least-bad mix is shown and flagged as not within the limit.
- **CVaR.** The average loss in the worst 5% of rolling 21-trading-day windows of the replay (95% level).
- **Target real return** is reported (met or missed, and by how much), never enforced.

## 6. Liquidity

- **Required cover** = spending over the safety-net window (indexed to inflation) plus any dated one-off outflows that fall inside it.
- **Available cover** = sum of each class's value x (1 - haircut). Haircuts: cash 0%, debt 8%, gold 15%, large-cap 30%, mid-cap 40%; illiquid
  assets count for nothing. Two **bases**: `all` (every class after its haircut) and `strict` (cash, debt and gold only). The basis is always chosen
  explicitly and shown.
- A **year-by-year ladder** (10 years) rolls the liquid sleeve forward at the portfolio's expected return after each year's outflows and reports how
  many months of spending the cover then represents.

## 7. Scenarios

Five presets (equities fall 25%; a stylised market crash; inflation and rate spike; a large unexpected expense of 8% of the corpus; a "lost decade"
that lowers the return by 4 points a year) and a build-your-own with six dials. A scenario applies fixed shocks to each class (and the illiquid
bucket), optional extra outflow and a return drag, then reports the change in value, whether it stays within the drawdown limit, the liquidity
cover afterwards (as a multiple of the requirement), and the real corpus at the horizon relative to today. **The shock sizes are fixed
placeholders**, not calibrated from data, and the crash preset is not a replay of any real year.

## 8. Plan, projection and explanations

- The **action plan** is the difference, per class, between the current holdings and a chosen mix, in rupees crore. It ignores tax, costs,
  settlement and market impact.
- The **projection** compounds the corpus at a constant return, less indexed spending and one-offs, and shows nominal and today's-rupee values.
- The **guided plan** ("Plan my money") turns six plain-language answers into the same profile inputs using fixed comfort levels (Protect, Balance,
  Grow) listed in protected-assumptions.md.
- The **Assistant** never calculates. Its answers are reviewed sentence templates filled with figures the engine already produced; a test fails if
  any digit appears in an answer without a registered source figure (ADR-0021).

## 9. What has been checked, and what has not

Checked (all in `tests/`, results in [testing.md](testing.md)):

- A **golden master** freezes the assumptions, the estimated inputs and every demo family's analysis, so a code change cannot move a number
  silently (`test_golden.py`); the HTTP API is compared with the same file (`test_api_parity.py`).
- **Invariants on random valid profiles** run through the API: weights add up and respect the limits, results are repeatable, renaming a profile
  changes no number, impossible inputs are refused with a reason (`test_financial_invariants.py`).
- Scenario arithmetic worked by hand for the liquidity-gap family (for example, a 25% equity fall costs exactly 137.5 crore there).

**Not** done, and not claimed:

- **No independent validation.** Nobody outside the project has reviewed the method or the assumptions. Passing tests shows the code does what was
  specified, not that the specification is right.
- **No out-of-sample test** (a walk-forward backtest was planned and not built) and **no cross-check** against a second optimiser such as
  PyPortfolioOpt (planned, not built).
- **No real market data**, and no calibration of the priors, haircuts, thresholds or shock sizes to any.
- Mean-variance optimisation is **sensitive to its inputs**; with a 50% prior and shrunk correlations the sample is stable, but a different data
  set or prior would give different mixes. Nothing here is "optimal" in any absolute sense; a mix is simply the one that scores highest on the stated objective *under these assumptions and these limits*.
- The replay uses constant weights with no trading costs, taxes or slippage, and smooths the illiquid bucket, so real drawdowns would look worse.
