# ADR-0008: Validation without ground truth

**Date**: 2026-10-17
**Status**: accepted
**Deciders**: Project owner, facilitated by Claude (grill-me session, Q11)

## Context

There is no ground truth for "the right allocation". The proposal lists validation of recommendations as an
open point. Claiming optimality would not survive scrutiny.

## Decision

Validate in layers and describe the tool as internally consistent and defensible, not optimal.
1. Correctness tests: hand-worked unit cases, a cross-check against PyPortfolioOpt on identical inputs, and
   tests that every constraint is respected.
2. Behavioural sanity tests: more risk aversion gives lower volatility, a stricter liquidity cover gives lower
   expected return, and no corner weights appear without a reason.
3. A historical walk-forward backtest against equal-weight and 60/40, labelled illustrative (stretch goal).
The mentor also reviews the three personas' outputs.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Backtest as the main proof
- **Pros**: Familiar to finance audiences.
- **Cons**: Costly to build, and past performance proves little.
- **Why not**: Demoted to stretch to pay for the multi-user work.

### Alternative 2: No external cross-check
- **Pros**: Fewer dependencies.
- **Cons**: A bug in the optimiser could go unnoticed.
- **Why not**: An independent library is cheap insurance.

## Consequences

### Positive
- A defensible, honest validation story and a test report generated by pytest.

### Negative
- No evidence of out-of-sample performance unless the stretch goal is reached.

### Risks
- Reviewers expect a backtest. Mitigation: explain the framing and keep the stretch goal ready if time allows.
